# Draft v4: mediator's bridging proposal (run 2, round 1)

Status: a mediator's text for round 1 of the run-2 negotiation. It is not agreed.
It starts from the run-1 final text (`final/statement_en.md`) and the nine verified dossiers in `research/`.
Brackets `[A | B]` mark points where the parties are still apart. The mediator's preferred option is listed first.

## The problem the text must solve

Seat count at about 90% counted (preliminary; official results due 4 Nov):

| Bloc | Seats |
|---|---|
| Netanyahu bloc | 53 |
| Zionist change bloc | 48 (Yashar 21 · Together 12 [7 Bennett, 5 Yesh Atid] · Democrats 8 · Yisrael Beiteinu 7) |
| Ra'am | 7 |
| Joint List | 12 (about 6 Hadash · 3 Ta'al · 3 Balad) |

Every non-Netanyahu government needs Ra'am to vote in favour and the Joint List not to vote against. Bennett, Yisrael Beiteinu and, in part, Yashar promised the opposite.
No wording removes that fact. The only question is whether some structure lets each party tell its own voters a true story it can live with.

## Structural options put to all nine parties

| Option | How it works | Who needs to move |
|---|---|---|
| **S1: Government guidelines + separate agreement** (run 1) | A government of 5 Zionist parties (48). Signed parliamentary agreement with Ra'am. Unilateral Joint List declaration. | Bennett and Yisrael Beiteinu break the pledge. Ra'am accepts being outside. |
| **S2: Policy, not a deal** | A government of 5 Zionist parties (48). Chapter D is government policy in the basic guidelines, adopted in its own right and not as a price. No signed text with any Arab party. Ra'am and the Joint List each decide unilaterally how to vote. | Ra'am votes yes with no signature, which is a big risk for it. The Joint List abstains or stays absent with no counter-commitment. Bennett and Yisrael Beiteinu still rely on Arab votes, but have signed nothing. |
| **S3: Ra'am in the coalition** | Yashar, Together, the Democrats, Yisrael Beiteinu and Ra'am (55). The Joint List abstains. | Bennett, Yisrael Beiteinu and Yashar (Ra'am would have to accept the four rules) all move furthest. |
| **S4: No change government** | A unity government with Likud without Netanyahu, or new elections. | Outside this framework. Each party should say honestly whether it prefers this. |

## Text (same clause numbers as run 1)

### Preamble
1. The text covers one Knesset term only. Unchanged.

2. The government. Two options:
   - **[S2]** A **Zionist, statist government** headed by Gadi Eisenkot, of Yashar, Together, Yisrael Beiteinu and the Democrats. It serves all citizens of Israel, Jews and Arabs alike.
   - **[S1 or S3]** As in run 1, plus the Ra'am arrangement in Chapter E.

3. Long-term questions are reserved. Unchanged.
   - New: Basic Law: Legislation will be enacted with broad consent.
   - New: a term limit for the Prime Minister.
   - Laws passed in the 25th Knesset that changed the judicial system:
     - **[A]** will be reviewed by a public committee, whose recommendations the government will bring to the Knesset within a year;
     - **[B]** will be repealed (the Democrats' position);
     - **[C]** no mention.

### Chapter A
4. Jewish and democratic. Two options:
   - **[A]** "The government parties affirm that the State of Israel is a Jewish and democratic state, as defined in its Basic Laws, and will act in the spirit of the Declaration of Independence…" The equality quote and the Nation-State Law reservation stay. The Arab parties do not sign this clause.
   - **[B]** The run-1 descriptive wording.

5. Arab citizens are equal citizens; their elected representatives are a legitimate part of Israeli democracy. Unchanged.
   - New: "The government will not initiate legislation to widen the grounds for disqualifying lists or candidates." This is Balad's demand, reduced to a commitment about future legislation.

6. Civil equality. Unchanged.

6a. **Service for All.** Rewritten so that no single population receives a special exemption:
   - **(a)** Within its first year the government will enact a law under which every young person liable under the Security Service Law serves, in the IDF or in national-civil service.
     - There will be no exemption quotas and no blanket exemptions.
     - Personal economic sanctions will apply to evaders.
     - The core curriculum will be a condition of state funding for education.
   - **(b)** State benefits tied to service go to those who serve, whoever they are. No existing universal right or allowance will be made conditional on service.
   - **(c)** For citizens who are not liable under the Security Service Law, national-civil service during this term is voluntary.
     - It is civilian, in their own communities, and run with the local authorities.
     - It expands through published incentives.
   - **(d)** If the law is not enacted within a year, the government will bring a decision on budgetary steps.

   This replaces the run-1 "protection for Arab citizens only". In practice Arab citizens are not called up today, and nothing in (a) changes that. Each party should say whether this is acceptable.

### Chapter B
7. All terror, and mourning for all innocent victims. Unchanged.

8. The 7 October massacre. Unchanged.

9. Hamas. Two options:
   - **[A]** "Hamas is a terrorist organization. It will be disarmed and removed from governing Gaza, and will not threaten the citizens of Israel." Ra'am records a reservation that it does not adopt the military wording.
   - **[B]** Run 1: "Hamas, a terrorist organization under Israeli law, will be disarmed…"

   Added: Israel's security freedom of action in Gaza is maintained under the international arrangement.

10. Jewish terror:
    - **[A]** Delete the phrase "same determination and same tools". Keep the list of tools: administrative detention, a dedicated Shin Bet and police unit, re-establishing the police district in Judea and Samaria, and prosecution. That keeps the substance without the equivalence slogan.
    - **[B]** Run 1.

    The closing line: "This violence is perpetrated by a small, violent minority (קומץ אלים), and it harms…"

11. A state commission of inquiry **within 14 days** of the government being sworn in.

### Chapter C
12. Status:
    - **[A]** "In Judea and Samaria (the West Bank), which have been held since 1967 under the authority of the military commander, the government will act as follows…" This states a fact about governance, not a definition of status. Clause 13 says the military commander's powers will be returned.
    - **[B]** Run 1: "territories held under military control".

13. No annexation, return of powers, and no money for outposts. Unchanged. Settlement construction:
    - **[A]** "No new communities will be established. In existing communities, planning and construction will proceed only under plans approved before 27 Oct 2026 and government decisions."
    - **[B]** Bennett: construction in existing communities continues by government decision.
    - **[C]** The Democrats: no expansion beyond approved plans, and E1 is frozen.

14. Outposts. Unchanged from run 1: evacuation, and no new legalization beyond government decisions taken before 27 Oct 2026.

15. Security and the future arrangement:
    - **[A]** "…between Israel and the Palestinians." The government will maintain security, civil and economic coordination with the Palestinian Authority.
    - **[B]** Hadash and Ta'al: "the Palestinian people", plus a commitment to renew a diplomatic process with the Palestinian leadership.

15a. Holy places. Unchanged.

### Chapter D
16. Crime: NIS 1bn a year.
    - The Shin Bet is involved against criminal organizations only, under law and judicial oversight. Hadash and Balad record their objection.
    - The plan is led by: **[A]** a government coordinator for the fight against crime in Arab society, appointed on the recommendation of the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads (outside the government); **[B]** a Ra'am deputy minister (S1/S3).

17. The Decision 550 successor, about NIS 30bn. Unchanged.

18. Kaminitz:
    - **[A]** Enforcement against existing inhabited homes is frozen. Within 6 months an amendment will suspend administrative fines and demolition orders against such homes until plans are approved, and set up a regularization track.
    - **[B]** Run 1: cancel.

19. The Negev. Recognition and regularization, with a first group within 18 months. Electricity and water.
    - **[A]** "During the process there will be no demolition of inhabited homes. Relocation, where needed, will take place only within an agreed regularization plan."
    - **[B]** Run 1: "no forced relocation".

20. Representation. Targets in the civil service.
    - **[A]** "The Knesset presidium and committees will be composed according to established practice and faction size." That gives the Joint List and Ra'am their customary posts without naming them.
    - **[B]** Run 1: named deputy Speakers for each list.

### Chapter E (S1 and S3 only)
21–25. Run 1, with these changes:
- Ra'am's vote on the Service Law is limited to clause 6a(a)–(b).
- The al-Aqsa status quo becomes a breach trigger.
- Monitoring committee:
  - **[A]** the government, the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads, and one representative of each Arab faction that has made a commitment (Ra'am, the Joint List), as **equal members**;
  - **[B]** run 1.

### Annex 1 (Joint List)
- **[A]** A bilateral exchange of letters. The PM-designate writes to the Joint List, listing Chapter D and clauses 5, 13 and 14 as government commitments. The Joint List replies with its safety-net commitment: it will not vote for no-confidence motions or dissolution, and on investiture and budgets it will be absent or abstain. This follows the 1992 model.
- **[B]** Run 1's unilateral declaration.
- Balad's terms: "absent" rather than "abstains"; and removal of Yisrael Beiteinu's public boycott line from Annex 2.

## The TV statement

At least six of the nine leaders reject the run-1 nine-leader joint reading in its current form, for opposite reasons. The options are:
- **T1:** a reduced joint text that all nine can read.
- **T2:** the government leaders read a joint statement, and the Arab leaders give their own statements, coordinated but separate.
- **T3:** each leader speaks separately; there is no joint text.

Each party must say which of these it can accept, and give the exact lines it must include or cannot say.
