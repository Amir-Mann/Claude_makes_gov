# Analysis of draft v1: design choices, gains and concessions, risks

Sources: `research/*.md`. Seat counts are preliminary (about 85% counted on 28 Oct 2026).

## 1. Arithmetic: why the design looks the way it does

| Bloc | Seats |
|---|---|
| Zionist opposition: Yashar 21, Together 12, Democrats 8, Yisrael Beiteinu 7 | **48** |
| Netanyahu bloc: Likud 26, Shas 7, UTJ 7, Otzma 7, RZP 6 | **53** |
| Ra'am | 7 |
| Joint List: Hadash + Ta'al about 9, Balad about 3 (inferred) | 12 |

- **Investiture and budgets** pass by a simple majority of MKs voting.
  - 48 + Ra'am 7 = 55, which beats 53.
  - But if Balad (about 3) voted *against*, the count would be 53 + 3 = 56, which beats 55. So Hadash and Ta'al must vote **for** the investiture. On the budget their abstention is enough provided Balad also abstains, which is its likely posture.
- **Constructive no-confidence** needs 61 MKs behind an alternative PM. The right has 53, so it cannot topple the government unless Arab parties join it, which none will.
  - Once formed, the government is **structurally stable**.
  - From then on its dependence on the Arab parties is limited to budgets and dissolution bills.
- **Conclusion:** no non-Netanyahu government exists without at least Ra'am voting yes and Hadash and Ta'al not voting no.
  - So Bennett's pledge ("not even reliance on abstention", 21 Oct) and Lieberman's ("not even a minority government", 15 Oct) **cannot both be kept** in any government without Netanyahu.
  - The one exception is a unity government with a Likud that has dropped Netanyahu: Yashar 21 + Together 12 + YB 7 + Likud 26 = 66.
  - That is the real **alternative** facing Bennett, Lieberman and Yashar (see §5).

## 2. Why Balad is excluded

- Balad's red line is "never inside / never vote for war budgets". The CEC also barred its chair over his 8 Oct 2023 article.
- Every Zionist partner voted, or would vote, to disqualify him, so including Balad would cost the Zionist side far more than it adds.
- The arithmetic works without Balad, provided it abstains.
- Balad's own stated posture (a "safety net" from outside, abstention) is compatible with that, so it can claim credit without signing.

## 3. Design principles used

1. **Describe the present; postpone the future.** Every identity clause is factual ("defined in its Basic Laws as…", "held under military control since 1967"). Clause 3 explicitly reserves every party's long-term positions, so nobody renounces anything.
2. **Symmetry.** Hamas's massacre (clause 8) and Jewish terror (clause 10) are condemned with equal force. The general civilian-harm clause (7) covers Gaza without naming it as an accusation.
3. **The Declaration of Independence as a bridge text.**
   - The Zionist parties cite it for "Jewish state".
   - The Arab parties cite it for "complete equality".
   - Yashar, Yesh Atid and Yisrael Beiteinu already use it.
4. **Gains in numbers, concessions in words.** The Arab parties' gains are quantified and monitored (Chapter D and clause 24). Their concessions are mostly declaratory, and phrased as descriptions rather than endorsements.
5. **A Zionist cabinet with a parliamentary agreement.** This follows the Rabin 1992 model. It lets Bennett, Lieberman and Yashar say "a Zionist government, no Arab ministers". It lets Ra'am, Hadash and Ta'al say "we toppled Netanyahu and got a signed, monitored package".

## 5. Reflection: is this a good statement, and could it be agreed?

**Strengths**
- On the user's four pillars, the language has precedents each party already used:
  - "Jewish terror": Lapid, Golan and reportedly Eisenkot.
  - Outpost evacuation: Bennett on 23 Jun.
  - "Jewish and democratic": Abbas in 2021.
  - Condemning the Oct 7 crimes: Abbas, Tibi.
- The Arab gains are concrete and measurable. The Zionist concessions on the four pillars are small.
- Clause 3 ("does not decide and does not prejudice") is the main safety valve. It lets Bennett keep his Area C plan, Hadash its 1967-state goal and Balad its "state of all citizens" goal, all without signing them.

**Weaknesses and honest doubts**
1. **The real cost is coalition shape, not the text.** Bennett and Lieberman pay their largest price simply by depending on Arab votes at all.
   - The text cannot remove that; it can only make it defensible: "they signed our Hamas and Jewish-state terms; no Arab ministers; a Zionist government".
   - If Likud without Netanyahu becomes available, Bennett, Lieberman and Yashar will probably prefer it, and this whole structure becomes moot. Today Netanyahu remains Likud's leader, so this is not on the table yet.
2. **The Hamas clause is the tightest knot.**
   - Yashar's rule 4 and Lieberman's test want "Hamas is a terror organization to be dismantled".
   - Abbas, Tibi and Hadash have not said this personally.
   - v1 uses "a massacre carried out by Hamas … these were acts of terror" plus "Hamas will not rule Gaza". That is probably the maximum the Arab parties can sign. It may be just below Yashar's minimum.
   - Possible bridge for v2: "Hamas, designated a terrorist organization under Israeli law, …" (descriptive, like clause 4).
3. **The political horizon (Hadash's top demand) is missing.** Only clause 15, "will not take steps that close off future options", gestures at it. Hadash may need more ("security and civil coordination with the Palestinian Authority", "the Palestinian people"), and Bennett and Lieberman resist exactly that.
4. **Service.** Yashar and Lieberman want "service for all" with sanctions. The Arab parties accept voluntary civil service only. Clause 21 sides with the Arab parties; Yashar may push back.
5. **Kaminitz and demolitions** versus Bennett's "enforcement against illegal Palestinian and Bedouin building". v1 freezes only residential demolitions where plans are pending.
6. **Language parity.** Hadash warned that the Hebrew and Arabic versions must match. Using both place names in each language (clause 12) tries to meet this.
7. **Budget dependency** lets Ra'am, Hadash and Ta'al hold the government hostage each year. Clause 25 makes this explicit, which is honest but gives the right an attack line.

**Overall:** I estimate the text itself is acceptable to six or seven of the eight signatories with minor wording changes. The likely holdouts are Hadash (political horizon) and Yashar or Yisrael Beiteinu (the Hamas wording).

The decisive obstacle is coalition structure, which no wording fully solves.
