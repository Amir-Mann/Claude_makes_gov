**Result of round 1: impasse.**
- Bennett and Yisrael Beiteinu (14 seats together) reject every structure that depends on Arab votes or abstention.
- Without both of them the change bloc has 41, and no government is possible.
- Their stated preference is a unity government with Likud without Netanyahu (S4a), or else new elections.
- Separately, the Arab parties' price has risen since run 1:
  - Ra'am wants a letter and its own post;
  - Hadash wants a horizon sentence;
  - Balad wants an anti-disqualification commitment.
  The Zionist right has moved the other way.

## Round 2 scenario (the mediator's assumption, to test whether anyone moves)

Assume the following, consistent with Likud's "stolen election" line on 28 Oct:
- Likud keeps Netanyahu.
- Netanyahu's own mandate fails: he has 53, and neither Bennett nor Lieberman joins him.
- So S4a is not available within the formation period.
- The real alternative is new elections around March 2027, with Netanyahu as caretaker prime minister until then and probably longer.

## Proposal P1: "Knesset first" (Yashar's track)

No government is formed now. From the swearing-in, the 67 MKs who oppose Netanyahu's continued rule each vote **separately and unilaterally** on four bills. Nobody signs anything with anybody.
1. Replace the Speaker.
2. A law setting up a state commission of inquiry into 7 October.
3. A law that an MK under indictment may not be tasked with forming a government (the "defendant law").
4. A Service for All law, as in clause 6a of v4.

**Question:** which of the four do you vote for?

**Consequence:** if bill 3 passes, Likud must choose another candidate. That may open S4a, a unity government without Netanyahu, and that government would not include Ra'am or the Joint List.
- **To the Arab parties:** do you still vote for bill 3, knowing it may produce a right-of-centre unity government without you?
- **To Bennett and Yisrael Beiteinu:** is voting alongside Ra'am and the Joint List on bills, with nothing signed, acceptable? (Your round-1 files say yes.)

## Proposal P2: the framework for the case where P1 fails

This is the case where Likud keeps Netanyahu even after bill 3, or bill 3 fails.

Architecture "S2+":
- A five-party government.
- No signed agreement with any faction.
- The PM-designate publishes the government's commitments to Arab society (Chapter D with numbers and dates, the crime post, the al-Aqsa breach clause) in an open public letter to the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads. This is Yashar's bridge.
- Ra'am and the Joint List each answer publicly and say how they will vote.

Does that letter meet:
- Ra'am's "matching letter" demand?
- Hadash's "signed, names the Joint List" demand?
- Ta'al's "safety net for our society" demand?

The horizon sentence goes in the same letter, not in the guidelines: "the government will maintain coordination with the Palestinian Authority and will take no step that precludes a future settlement". Hadash wanted "between Israel and the Palestinian people" and "renew a diplomatic process"; the mediator's draft leaves both out.

Crime post: Yoav Segalovitz (Ra'am No. 2, a Jewish former police major-general who meets the four rules in person) as **government coordinator with powers delegated by the Minister of Public Security**. He is not a deputy minister, and sits outside the government.

**Question to Bennett and Yisrael Beiteinu:** faced with the choice between new elections under Netanyahu as caretaker and S2+, which do you pick? Lieberman forced repeat elections in 2019 rather than compromise; Bennett formed a government with Ra'am in 2021. Answer as the real leader would, knowing his record.
