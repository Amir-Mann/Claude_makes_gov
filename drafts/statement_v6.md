# Draft v6: consolidated text for the closing round (run 2, round 3)

Rounds 1 and 2 of run 2 changed the shape of the document:
- There is **no signed agreement between the government and any Arab faction.**
- Chapter D of run 1 becomes an **open letter from the PM-designate** to the heads of the Arab local authorities. The government adopts it as a decision at its first meeting.
- Ra'am and the Joint List each answer the letter **separately, in public.** Their replies set their own conditions, including the al-Aqsa condition.
- Alongside this there is a **"Knesset first" track** (Part C): four bills on which the factions vote separately.

Sections marked ⟦…⟧ are the mediator's compromise on the points still open after round 2.

## Part A. Basic guidelines of the government (signatories: Yashar, Together, Yisrael Beiteinu, the Democrats)

**Preamble**

Whereas the 26th Knesset election did not produce a majority for a government headed by Benjamin Netanyahu;

Whereas the signatory factions hold different, and at times opposite, views on questions of principle, including the final status of Judea and Samaria, the constitutional arrangement of the state, and the national aspirations of its citizens;

Whereas the signatories wish to form a government for one Knesset term, to restore orderly government after 7 October and to repair what can be repaired in four years;

the following has been agreed:

1. A government will be formed, headed by MK Gadi Eisenkot, with the factions Yashar, Together, Yisrael Beiteinu and the Democrats as members.
   It is a Zionist, statist government, and it will serve all citizens of Israel, Jews and Arabs alike. The vote of every citizen carries equal weight.

2. These guidelines do not decide questions of principle, and nothing in them waives any party's position on them. No irreversible step will be taken on such questions during the term.
   Matters not settled here, including religion and state, will be settled in the coalition agreements.

**The state and its citizens**

3. The government parties affirm that the State of Israel is a Jewish and democratic state, as defined in the Basic Laws. The government will act in the spirit of the Declaration of Independence, which promises "complete equality of social and political rights to all its inhabitants irrespective of religion, race or sex".
   Nothing in this clause binds any party to a position on Basic Law: Israel – the Nation-State of the Jewish People, and each party may act on that matter according to law.

4. The Arab citizens of Israel are citizens with equal rights. Their elected representatives are a legitimate part of Israeli democracy.
   ⟦The government will not initiate legislation to widen the grounds for disqualifying lists or candidates from standing for the Knesset.⟧

5. During the term the government will not initiate legislation that harms the civil equality of any group of citizens, and will not make civil rights conditional on political views.
   Government services will be available in Arabic in full.

6. **Service for all.** Based on the principle of equal burden, within its first year the government will pass a law with the following elements:
   - a. Every young person liable under the Security Service Law will serve, in the IDF or in national-civil service.
     - There will be no exemption quotas and no blanket exemptions.
     - Personal economic sanctions will apply to evaders and will take effect on the dates set in the law.
     - Meeting the core curriculum will be a condition of state funding for educational institutions.
   - b. Benefits tied to service will go to those who serve, whoever they are. No existing universal right or allowance will be made conditional on service.
   - c. For citizens not liable under the Security Service Law, national-civil service during this term is voluntary.
     - It will be civilian, in their own communities, and run with the local authorities.
     - The law will not change the existing call-up arrangements.
   - d. If the law has not passed within a year, the government will decide on budgetary steps against institutions whose students evade service, and on suspending benefits tied to service.

7. The government will promote Basic Law: Legislation, with broad agreement, and legislation limiting the Prime Minister's term of office.
   The laws on the judicial system passed in the 25th Knesset will be reviewed by a public committee, which will report within a year.
   ⟦Until the government decides on the committee's report, the powers created by those laws will not be exercised. Returning to the arrangements that applied before those laws is not a change that requires broad agreement.⟧

**Security and the fight against terror**

8. The government parties condemn all terror and every deliberate attack on innocent civilians, whoever the perpetrator and whoever the victim. They grieve for all the innocent civilians killed since 7 October, Israelis and Palestinians alike, including in the Gaza Strip.

9. The government parties condemn without reservation the massacre Hamas carried out on 7 October 2023: the murder, rape and abduction of civilians.

10. Hamas is a terrorist organization.
    - Its military and governing capabilities will be dismantled. It will not rule the Gaza Strip and will not threaten the citizens of Israel.
    - Israel keeps its freedom of security action, in coordination with the international framework for Gaza.
    - The government will act to secure the end of the war, to return any remaining hostages and the bodies of the fallen, and to enable humanitarian aid and reconstruction for the civilian population.

11. **Jewish terror.** The government will fight Jewish terror and nationalist violence against Palestinians and their property with the same determination with which the state fights all terror. It will use:
    - administrative detention under law and judicial review;
    - a joint Shin Bet and police unit;
    - re-establishment of the Judea and Samaria police district;
    - dismantling of violent hubs established without permission;
    - prosecution.

    This violence is the work of a small, violent minority, and it harms Palestinians, the residents of the area and the State of Israel itself.

12. Within 14 days of its swearing-in, the government will establish a state commission of inquiry into the events of 7 October, the decade before them, and the conduct of the war.

**Judea and Samaria (the West Bank)**

13. In Judea and Samaria, which have been held under the authority of the military commander since 1967, the government will act as follows during the term:
    - a. There will be no annexation, no application of sovereignty and no change in legal status.
    - b. Powers transferred from the military commander to political appointees will be returned to the IDF and the Civil Administration.
    - c. ⟦No new communities will be established. Construction in existing communities will take place within their approved plans and by government decision.⟧
    - d. The state budget will include no allocations to unauthorized outposts and no coalition money for them.

14. Outposts and "farms" established without a government decision and without legal authorization will be evacuated, first and foremost those on private Palestinian land. A timetable will be published within 90 days, and progress will be reported every quarter.
    No unauthorized outpost will be regularized during the term, except for regularization steps decided by the government before 27 October 2026. Those will proceed under law and judicial review, and none on private Palestinian land.

15. Israel keeps full security responsibility and freedom of security action throughout the area.
    The government will maintain security and civil coordination with the Palestinian Authority, and will work with regional and international partners for stability.
    In accordance with clause 2, no decision on a permanent settlement will be taken during the term, and the parties' positions on it are reserved in full.

16. The government will preserve the historic status quo at the holy places in Jerusalem, including on the Temple Mount (al-Aqsa).

17. The commitments in the open letter of the PM-designate (Part B) will be approved as a government decision at the government's first meeting, and will be funded in the first state budget.

**Reservations by signatories**
- Together (Bennett): a free vote on candidates for the Knesset presidium.
- Yisrael Beiteinu: will not count on the votes of the Joint List. ⟦This line will not appear in the signed document; Yisrael Beiteinu will make it in its own statements.⟧
- The Democrats: clause 13c means no expansion beyond approved plans, and no regularization of outposts on state land.

## Part B. Open letter of the PM-designate to the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads

⟦A copy goes to the chair of the High Follow-Up Committee.⟧ It is published before the investiture vote and annexed to the basic guidelines.

> The government I am forming will act for all citizens of Israel. I am setting out here, in public, what it commits to do for Arab society in its four years. These are commitments to citizens and their elected local leaders, not a price for votes. I invite you to judge us on them every quarter.

1. **Crime and violence.**
   - A national plan with at least NIS 1 billion a year, entered into the budget baseline for four years.
   - It covers policing, prevention and enforcement against criminal organizations, including the Shin Bet against those organizations only, under law and judicial review.
   - It will be led by a **government coordinator for the fight against crime and violence in Arab society.**
     - The coordinator is appointed by government decision, with written powers delegated by the Minister of Public Security over the plan's budget, and can be removed only by the government.
     - ⟦The government intends to appoint Yoav Segalovitz, who will resign from the Knesset before taking up the post.⟧
   - An annual report will be published on homicides and the rate of cases solved and charged.

2. **Economic plan.**
   - A five-year plan to follow Government Decision 550, no smaller than it in real terms (about NIS 30 billion), approved in the first budget.
   - Grants to Arab local authorities that were cut will be restored.
   - It will have published targets for employment, Hebrew teaching and higher education, placement of Arab teachers, medical residency posts, and housing and jobs for young people.

3. **Planning and housing.**
   - Master plans for Arab localities on a published timetable, and expansion of their jurisdictions.
   - From the day the government is sworn in, enforcement under Amendment 116 to the Planning and Building Law (the "Kaminitz Law") against existing inhabited homes will be frozen.
   - Within 6 months an amendment will suspend the administrative fines and administrative demolition orders against such homes until plans are approved, and will create a regularization track. This includes the Triangle and the Negev: Tayibe, Tira, Qalansawe and the Negev localities among them.
   - Enforcement against new construction and against criminal organizations will continue.

4. **The Negev.**
   - A recognition and regularization process for the unrecognized villages, with recognition decisions for a first group within 18 months, and connection to electricity and water.
   - During the process there will be no demolition of inhabited homes. Relocation, where needed, will happen only under a regularization plan agreed with the residents and their elected representatives.

5. **Representation.**
   - Targets for Arab representation in the civil service and on the boards of government companies.
   - The Knesset presidium and committees will be composed according to established practice and faction size. The government parties will not block the election of a deputy Speaker from any faction.

6. **Monitoring.**
   - A monitoring committee chaired by the coordinator will meet every month and publish a report every quarter.
   - Members: representatives of the government, the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads, and ⟦one MK from each Knesset faction that publicly responded to this letter, as equal members⟧.

7. **The holy places and the horizon.**
   - The government will preserve the historic status quo at the holy places in Jerusalem, including al-Aqsa.
   - It will maintain coordination with the Palestinian Authority and take no step that precludes a future settlement ⟦between Israel and the Palestinians⟧. It will respond to regional and international efforts toward stability and a settlement.

## Part D. Replies of the Arab factions (unilateral; the government is not a party to them)

- **Ra'am:** will vote for investiture and for budgets that fund Part B. It reserves the right to withdraw support if:
  - the status quo at al-Aqsa is breached; or
  - Part B is breached and the breach is not remedied within 60 days.
- **Joint List:** "we did not vote for the government". Hadash and Ta'al abstain on investiture; Balad's MKs are absent. While Part B is implemented, the list will not support no-confidence motions or dissolution. The safety net lapses after a breach of Part B or of the al-Aqsa status quo that is not remedied within 60 days.
  - Each component keeps its own positions on occupation, the Palestinian people and service, as stated in its reply.
