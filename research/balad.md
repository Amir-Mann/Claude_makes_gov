## 1. Identity

  withdrawal. Balad withdrew his candidacy that night and filed it on 2 Oct, so the court issued no ruling on him. The court unanimously

    the court bowed to Ben-Gvir. https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/3/israel-election-double-standard-arab-leader-forced-out-far-right-cleared
- **List:** Balad runs inside the **Joint List (القائمة المشتركة / הרשימה המשותפת)** with Hadash and Ta'al. The three parties agreed the

- **Base:** Palestinian-nationalist Arab citizens: the educated secular and nationalist middle class, youth, students, and mixed cities
  (Jaffa, Haifa). It has a meaningful Christian and Druze-secular component. When it ran alone in 2022 it won 2.9% and missed the threshold.

- **Ideology in 3 lines:** a Palestinian Arab nationalist, democratic and secular party. It wants Israel transformed from a Jewish state into
  "a state of all its citizens" (دولة جميع مواطنيها / מדינת כל אזרחיה), with Arabs recognised as an indigenous national minority that has
  cultural and educational autonomy. It wants a full end to the 1967 occupation and Palestinian refugee return, and it refuses on principle
  to join Zionist governments.

## 1b. Ideological foundations (long-held, whole-career positions)

1. **Founding (1995–96) under Azmi Bishara.** Bishara wrote and popularised the formula that Israel should be "a state of all its citizens"
   rather than a Jewish state. The party's strategy is cultural autonomy for Arabs as a national minority, to be used as leverage to change
   the character of the state. Balad first ran with Hadash in 1996, then with Ta'al in 1999 (2 seats), then alone from 2003 to 2013
   (3 seats each time). It joined the Joint List in 2015.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balad_(political_party) ; https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/balad-political-party ;
   https://en.idi.org.il/israeli-elections-and-parties/parties/balad/
2. **Platform pillars (party documents, as summarised by IDI and Polimeter from the 2018 platform):**
   - full Israeli withdrawal from the territories occupied in 1967;
   - a Palestinian state with Jerusalem as its capital;
   - the Palestinian right of return;
   - recognition of Arabs as a national minority with autonomy in education, culture and media;
   - repeal of the special status of Zionist institutions (Jewish Agency and JNF);
   - complete separation of religion and state;
   - constitutional equality between citizens and between national groups;
   - official status for Arabic;
   - an Arab university.
   Wikipedia also describes Balad as having one-state and right-of-return leanings. In practice the party's line has been
   "end the occupation + a state of all citizens inside", and its electoral documents back the 1967 two-state framework.
   https://polimeter.co.il/parties/balad ; https://en.idi.org.il/israeli-elections-and-parties/parties/balad/
3. **Refusal to legitimise Zionist governments.** Balad has voted against every state budget since its founding (Wikipedia). In Sept 2019
   Balad's 3 MKs refused to recommend Gantz: only 10 of the 13 Joint List MKs recommended him. In March 2020 Balad **did** join the full
   Joint List recommendation of Gantz. This is the key precedent that Balad can recommend a non-Netanyahu candidate when blocking the far
   right is at stake. In Sept 2022 Balad left the Joint List over the recommendation and coordination question. IDI summarises Balad's
   conditions then as no PM recommendation, no coordination with other parties and no coalition membership. Abu Shehadeh accused Lapid of
   engineering the split.
   https://www.jns.org/joint-list-clarifies-only-10-mks-back-blue-and-white-not-13/ ; https://www.jpost.com/Israel-Elections/Balad-members-oppose-recommending-Gantz-602520 ;
   https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001321919 ; https://www.timesofisrael.com/balad-leader-accuses-lapid-or-orchestrating-split-with-joint-list/ ; https://idi.org.il//en.idi.org.il/articles/45950
4. **Disqualification history.** The CEC banned Balad in 2003 and 2009, and banned Balad again in 2022 on the ground that it "rejects Israel's
   existence as a Jewish and democratic state". The Supreme Court overturned every ban. 2026 is the first time the party's leader was
   effectively removed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balad_(political_party)
5. **Defining controversies (shape how Zionist partners see Balad):**
   - In Feb 2016 MKs Zahalka, Ghattas and Zoabi met families of attackers who had been killed. They said the purpose was to get the bodies
     returned. The Ethics Committee suspended them. https://thejerusalemfund.org/?p=6782
   - Ex-MK Basel Ghattas was jailed for 2 years (2017) for smuggling phones to security prisoners under a plea deal.
     https://www.timesofisrael.com/ex-mk-ghattas-sentenced-to-2-years-in-jail-as-court-okays-plea-deal/
   - Azmi Bishara left Israel in 2007 under suspicion of aiding Hezbollah in 2006. Hanin Zoabi was on the Mavi Marmara in 2010.
     **[unverified-this-session]**
   - Abu Shehadeh's 8 Oct 2023 article, which led to the 2026 disqualification (section 4b).
6. **Divergence flags (recent vs. long-held):**
   - **Outside support for a government.** In 2026 Balad joined a list whose chair openly offers a "safety net" / outside support for a
     non-Netanyahu government (JNS, 12 Oct 2026). In June 2026 Abu Shehadeh himself said a PM candidate who approaches the list would be
     "considered". This departs from the 2019/2022 "recommend no one" line. It is consistent with 2020, but it is a real softening.
   - **Admitting fault over Oct 7.** In court (Oct 2026) Abu Shehadeh conceded "The article contains things that were wrong" and "I would
     not say them today". This is a first for him on Oct 7. He still would not produce any statement calling Hamas a terrorist
     organisation: asked for one, he said "אין מאמר כזה" ("there is no such article").
   - **Pragmatism framed as necessity.** Joining the list was framed as a "technical framework" accepted "to prevent the return of the
     fascist right-wing coalition". At the same time, Balad's own cadre insists the list is a "national identity project" (Al Jazeera,
     25 May 2026). The tension between the two framings is visible.

## 4. Positions on statement topics

**(a) Jewish state / Nation-State Law.**
- Abu Shehadeh (ynet, ≈Jun 2026; Q) on "Jewish and democratic": "זה אוקסימורון. זה לא תקין וזה לא עובד" ("It's an oxymoron. It's not right
  and doesn't work"). Also: "אנחנו מאמינים שישראל צריכה להיות מדינת כל אזרחיה" ("Israel should be a state of all its citizens").
- He frames "state of all its citizens" as ending "Jewish supremacy" in legislation (JPost, Sep 2026; P). https://www.jpost.com/israel-election-2026/article-907154
- The Nation-State Law is "العنصري" (racist) and must be replaced by a Basic Law: Equality.
- Implication: **Balad cannot sign "we recognize/affirm Israel as a Jewish state."** At most it could tolerate a descriptive clause
  attributed to the government or the coalition ("the government operates under Israel's Basic Laws…"), if Balad is not a coalition
  member and is not the signatory. Even that is costly for its voters.

**(b) Terrorism, Hamas, Oct 7, Gaza, hostages.**
- The 8 Oct 2023 article, as quoted by Otzma's petition (ynetnews), called the attack "an important historical event from a military,
  political and strategic perspective". Otzma also attributed to it "If small and besieged Gaza succeeded in doing this, others can do it
  more efficiently and simply". https://www.ynetnews.com/article/yrsnwoll2
- 2026 walk-back:
  - To the court (Q, JPost): "The article contains things that were wrong", "I would not say them today, and I should not have written it
    that way", "I have never called for violence, and I never will".
  - To the CEC (Arab48, 18 Sep 2026; P/Q): he says that before Oct 7 and throughout the war he repeatedly condemned harm to innocents
    "من اليهود والعرب على حد سواء" ("Jews and Arabs alike").
  - The JPost analysis says he later "condemned the massacre". https://www.arab48.com/محليات/سياسة/2026/09/18/
- **He has never labelled Hamas a terrorist organisation.** In court, asked for such an article (Q): "אין מאמר כזה".
- The Joint List platform does not use the word "terrorism" and frames Gaza as "genocide".
- Hostages: no 2026 Balad statement was found. Historically the party called for the return of all hostages and prisoners within a
  ceasefire framework [unverified-this-session].
- Implication: "condemns all terrorism, Hamas explicitly" is the hardest clause for Balad. A formula condemning "all harm to civilians,
  Israeli and Palestinian", or "the 7 October massacre of civilians and the killing of civilians in Gaza", is in principle signable by
  Balad, but only as part of a symmetric text. An explicit "Hamas is a terrorist organisation" would be a political liability with its
  base, even after the court episode.

**(c) Arab citizens.**
- **Crime is the top priority.** Q (ynet, ≈Jun 2026): "הפכנו להיות אחת החברות הכי מסוכנות בעולם" ("We've become one of the most dangerous

- **Planning and housing:** stop demolitions, repeal the Kaminitz Law, expand town master plans. **Negev:** "rights of displaced people";

- **Representation:** recognition as an "indigenous national minority"; Arabic's status; legitimacy of Arab parties; an end to incitement

**(d) West Bank.**
- End the occupation, with a state on the 1967 lines and East Jerusalem as its capital. Opposes annexation, settlement and settler violence.

## 6. Negotiation profile (in role, as Balad)

**Position:** Balad's 3 (probable) MKs sit inside a 12-seat Joint List. Balad will not join the coalition. Its realistic maximum is
supporting outside support (a Rabin-1992-style "safety net") or a recommendation to the President, and only if it can show its voters
gains that are concrete, measurable and national, not only budgetary.

**MUST-HAVES**
1. Netanyahu, Ben-Gvir and Smotrich out. This is the mandate.
2. A funded, monitored, multi-year plan against crime and violence, with Arab partnership and civilian policing, not "Shin Bet-isation".
3. Stop home demolitions, freeze or repeal Kaminitz enforcement, and real planning, including the Negev unrecognised villages.
4. Recognition of Arab citizens as equal citizens and of the Arab parties as legitimate. An end to disqualification and incitement
   campaigns.
5. No votes for war or settlement budgets. Balad votes only on measures it supports, issue by issue.
6. A "political horizon": explicit language on the occupation (or at least "military control"), settler terrorism, outpost evacuation,
   and no annexation.

**Nice-to-haves**
- Restoring and expanding the Decision 550 successors.
- Status for Arabic.
- An opening to amend or repeal the Nation-State Law, or a Basic Law: Equality.
- An Arab university.
- Al-Aqsa status quo.
- Lifting the Gaza siege, aid and reconstruction.
- Reform of disqualification rules (Section 7A).

**Possible concessions**
- Not demanding "state of all its citizens" language in the text.
- Tolerating a descriptive mention of the Basic Laws or the Declaration of Independence that Balad does not itself affirm.
- Accepting "territories under military control" instead of "occupation" **if** settler terrorism and outposts are explicit.
- Condemning harm to all civilians, including the Oct 7 massacre, in symmetric language.
- Staying silent on the end-state (one state vs. two states).
- Giving outside support or abstaining on confidence votes rather than voting yes.

**Deal-breakers**
- Balad's own signature affirming "Israel as the nation-state of the Jewish people" or endorsing the Nation-State Law.
- A one-sided terrorism clause naming only Palestinians, combined with silence on settler terror or Gaza civilians.
- Commitment to continued war or "destroying Hamas" as a government goal Balad must back.
- Settlement or annexation budgets.
- Service obligations imposed on Arab citizens.
- A deal where Arab votes are used but Arab parties are publicly delegitimised (Bennett/Lieberman "no reliance" rhetoric).

**What Balad needs to show voters:** that the Arab vote toppled the "government of bloodshed" and bought tangible results: crime falling,
demolitions stopping, budgets flowing. It must also show that Balad did not sign away Palestinian identity: no "Jewish state" oath, no
denial of the Nakba, no one-sided "terror" text, and "occupation" or at least "military control" plus settler terrorism named.
After its chair was disqualified, Balad is especially sensitive to humiliation. Any text that reads as a loyalty test will be rejected
by its central committee.
