# Final assessment (run 2): how realistic is this?

## Bottom line
**No four-year government of this bloc can be formed on the current numbers.** The run-1 result, nine "yes" answers in three rounds, came from the brief, which told every agent to be "supportive of a middle ground". It did not come from the parties. When each movement argued as its real negotiators would, the outcome split into three:

| Track | Result |
|---|---|
| A government of Yashar, Together, Yisrael Beiteinu and the Democrats, resting on Ra'am's votes and the Joint List's abstention (Parts A and B) | **Not agreed.** Seven movements could live with the text. Bennett and Yisrael Beiteinu refuse, and without their 14 seats the change bloc is 41 against 53. |
| Monitoring committee (Part B, clause 6) | **Open even among the seven.** Ra'am, Hadash and Ta'al demand equal membership for the Arab factions; Yashar and Yisrael Beiteinu offer reports and questions only. |
| "Knesset first": bills voted on separately (Part C) | **Agreed by all nine** on three bills: a new Speaker, a state commission into 7 October, and a bar on an MK under indictment forming a government. Six back a service-for-all law; the Joint List votes against it. |
| A single TV statement read by all nine leaders | **Not achievable.** Replaced by three separate statements on the same evening: the government leaders, Ra'am, and the Joint List. |

Every agent's own estimate in the closing round was the same. The Knesset-first bills pass. Then either Likud replaces Netanyahu and a unity government without the Arab parties forms, or there are elections around March 2027 with Netanyahu as caretaker.

## The numbers (preliminary, about 90% counted on 28 Oct; official results due 4 Nov)
Likud 26 · Yashar 21 · Joint List 12 · Together 12 (7 Bennett, 5 Yesh Atid) · Democrats 8 · Ra'am 7 · Yisrael Beiteinu 7 · Shas 7 · UTJ 7 · Otzma 7 · RZP 6.
- Netanyahu bloc 53 · Zionist change bloc 48 · Arab lists 19.
- Change bloc plus Ra'am = 55 against 53. That passes only if the Joint List does not vote against. If Balad's 3 vote no, it fails 55 against 56.
- Without Bennett and Yisrael Beiteinu the change bloc is 41, and no structure works.

## Assumptions from run 1 that were wrong
1. **Bennett would sign with a reservation.** His 2026 pledge covers a government *supported* by Arab parties, including outside support. Run 1 read his silence on election night as flexibility. Every report from 27–28 Oct has him still ruling it out.
2. **Lieberman would accept Ra'am from outside.** That idea came from an anonymous activist quoted in Haaretz. His own words were "Zionist parties only" and "לא ניתן להישען על ח"כים מרע"ם ומהרשימה המשותפת" (19 Oct).
3. **Ra'am would vote for a "Service for All" law that applies to Arab citizens.** Abbas backed it in May 2026 and retreated after August. By 26 Oct he was calling conscription talk «إشاعات وافتراءات» ("rumours and slanders").
4. **The nine leaders could share a stage.** On 23 September the whole Zionist opposition voted, in a 31–4 decision, in the Central Elections Committee to disqualify Balad's chair, Sami Abu Shehadeh. Run 1 never mentioned this. It shapes everything Balad does, and why Yashar and Bennett won't stand with Balad.
5. **Yashar was the largest party.** Likud is. Eisenkot himself said on 25 Oct that the head of the largest party is the automatic candidate.
6. **The Hamas wording would carry.** Yashar's own rule is "ארגון טרור שצריך להשמיד אותו" (a terrorist organization that must be destroyed). Run 1's "disarmed" was weaker than the Zionist parties' lines and stronger than anything Ra'am will say in the first person.
7. **Details.**
   - Balad's third MK is Hassan al-Nasasra, not "Nahaya Washahi".
   - Awawdeh is not a confirmed faction head.
   - Several run-1 quotes were reporters' wording or 2022 material presented as 2026 statements. Each dossier's verification log in `research/<slug>.md`, section 0, marks every claim CONFIRMED, CORRECTED, UNVERIFIED or LIKELY INVENTED.
8. **Structure.** Run 1 treated "concessions in words, gains in numbers" as the solution. In run 2 the binding constraint was not wording at all. It was arithmetic and personal pledges made in the last two months of the campaign.

## What would change the outcome
- **Likud drops Netanyahu,** perhaps after the defendant law (Part C, bill 3). Bennett, Yisrael Beiteinu and Yashar then prefer a unity government of 66 to 74 seats without the Arab parties. Part A could be reused as its guidelines, without Part B's commitments to the Arab factions.
- **A repeat election produces no Zionist majority again.** Lieberman's 2020 record suggests he might then accept a government resting on Arab support. Bennett set four conditions for moving and gave it about a 20% chance.
- **The double-envelope count moves a seat or two.** The arithmetic above does not change.

## Method and limits
- **Research.** Nine movement agents researched independently in Hebrew, Arabic and English, and each verified the run-1 dossier claim by claim. A shared web-search quota ran out partway through. Unverified items are marked as such; nothing was filled in from guesswork.
- **Rounds.** There were three negotiation rounds:
  1. structures S1–S4;
  2. the "moment of truth", testing elections against an Arab-supported government;
  3. closing, with locked wording.
- **Agents are still agents.** They argued harder this time, but they cannot read polls in real time or feel personal rivalries. Their forecasts are reasoned guesses.
- **The Hebrew and Arabic texts** were written by drafter agents working from verbatim models of real agreements (`research/STYLE_GUIDE.md`) and checked by back-translation. They are not reviewed by human native drafters.
