# Final assessment: how realistic is this?

## Result of the simulation
- **All 9 movements reached a "yes":**
  - 5 sign the framework itself: Yashar, Together (counted twice, for Bennett and Yesh Atid), Yisrael Beiteinu and The Democrats.
  - Ra'am signs the parliamentary agreement.
  - Hadash, Ta'al and Balad sign the unilateral safety-net declaration (Annex 1).
- **It took 3 review rounds.** Round 1 produced 8 "accept with changes" and 1 "reject" (Yisrael Beiteinu). Round 2 produced 9 accepts, each with at most one change. In round 3, all 9 signed.

## Why it worked: three structural moves
1. **Describe, don't endorse.** Every identity clause is phrased as a description of current law, and long-term positions are explicitly reserved.
2. **Concessions in words, gains in numbers.** The Zionist parties concede vocabulary and outside dependence. The Arab parties concede symbolic endorsements, which are mostly disclaimed.
3. **Three tiers of commitment.** These are the cabinet, a parliamentary agreement with Ra'am, and a unilateral declaration by the Joint List. Each party signs only as much as its base can bear.

## Honest caveats
- **Simulated agents converge more easily than politicians.** Each agent was told to be "supportive of a middle ground". Real parties also weigh personal rivalry, polls, media cycles and their alternatives.
- **The alternative of a unity government with Likud without Netanyahu** (Yashar + Together + Yisrael Beiteinu + Likud = 66) would dominate this deal for Bennett, Lieberman and possibly Eisenkot if Likud ever dropped Netanyahu. Yisrael Beiteinu explicitly wants to test it first.
- **Fragility.** The government lives only while Ra'am votes yes and the whole Joint List, including Balad, does not vote no. Every annual budget is a hostage moment.
- **Hardest real-world pressure points:**
  - Bennett's personal promise not to rely on Arab parties.
  - Ra'am's binding vote on the Haredi draft.
  - Hadash and Ta'al accepting a text with no "political horizon".
- **Preliminary data.** Seat counts were preliminary (about 85% counted on 28 Oct 2026). Several quotes in the research files are paraphrases or come from search summaries; each file's section 7 lists what could not be verified.
- **Not authoritative translations.** The Hebrew and Arabic versions were written by the coordinator. Before any real use they should be reviewed by native-speaking political drafters, especially for legal terms.
