# Four Years of Repair: Basic Guidelines and Open Letter, 2026–2030

**Status at the close of negotiations, 29 October 2026: not agreed.**

This is the text as it stood after three rounds. Seven of the nine movements could live with it:
- Yashar, Yesh Atid and the Democrats would sign Part A.
- Ra'am would vote for the government.
- Hadash, Ta'al and Balad would let it stand.

Bennett's party and Yisrael Beiteinu reject any government whose investiture depends on Arab votes or abstention. Without their 14 seats the change bloc has 41, so this government cannot be formed.

One point is also still open among the seven: membership of the monitoring committee (Part B, clause 6).

The one part all nine movements agreed to is Part C, the "Knesset first" bills, on which each faction votes separately.

## Part A. Basic guidelines of the government

Whereas the election to the 26th Knesset did not produce a majority for a government headed by Benjamin Netanyahu;

And whereas the undersigned factions hold different, and at times opposite, positions on questions of principle, among them the final status of Judea and Samaria, the constitutional order of the state and the national aspirations of its citizens;

And whereas the undersigned wish to form a government for one Knesset term, to restore orderly government after 7 October and to repair what can be repaired in four years;

It is therefore agreed as follows:

1. A government will be formed under MK Gadi Eisenkot, with the factions Yashar, Together, Yisrael Beiteinu and the Democrats as its members. It will be a Zionist, statist government and will serve all citizens of Israel, Jews and Arabs alike. Every citizen's vote carries equal weight.

   *Bennett's party and Yisrael Beiteinu reject Part A (see the status note above).*

2. These guidelines decide no question of principle, and no party waives its position on any such question. The government will take no irreversible step on these questions during its term. Matters not settled here, religion and state among them, will be settled in the coalition agreements.

### The state and its citizens

3. The government parties affirm that the State of Israel is a Jewish and democratic state, as defined in the Basic Laws. The government will act in the spirit of the Declaration of Independence, which promises "complete equality of social and political rights to all its inhabitants irrespective of religion, race or sex." Nothing in this clause binds a party to any position on Basic Law: Israel – the Nation-State of the Jewish People, and each party may act on that law in accordance with the law.

4. The Arab citizens of Israel are citizens with equal rights, and their elected representatives are a legitimate part of Israeli democracy. The government will not initiate legislation to widen the grounds for disqualifying lists or candidates from standing for the Knesset.

5. The government will not initiate legislation that harms the civil equality of any group of citizens, and will not make civil rights conditional on political views. Government services will be fully available in Arabic.

6. Service for all. Within its first year the government will bring about the enactment of a law based on equal sharing of the burden:
   - a. Every young person liable under the Security Service Law will serve, in the IDF or in national-civil service. There will be no exemption quotas and no blanket exemptions. Personal economic sanctions will apply to evaders from the dates set in the law. State funding of educational institutions will depend on teaching the core curriculum.
   - b. Benefits linked to service will go to those who serve, whoever they are. No existing universal right or allowance will be made conditional on service.
   - c. For citizens not liable under the Security Service Law, national-civil service in this term will be voluntary, civilian and in their own communities, and will be run with the local authorities. The law will not change existing call-up arrangements.
   - d. If the law has not been passed within a year, the government will decide on budgetary measures against institutions whose students evade service, and on suspending benefits linked to service.

7. The government will advance Basic Law: Legislation, with broad agreement, and a law limiting the Prime Minister's term of office. The laws on the judicial system passed by the 25th Knesset will be reviewed by a public committee, which will report within a year. Until the government decides on the committee's report, the powers created by those laws will not be exercised. A return to the arrangements that preceded those laws is not a change requiring broad agreement.

### Security and the fight against terror

8. The government parties condemn all terror and every deliberate attack on innocent civilians, whoever the perpetrator and whoever the victim. They grieve for every innocent civilian killed since 7 October, Israelis and Palestinians alike, including in the Gaza Strip.

9. The government parties condemn without reservation the massacre that Hamas carried out on 7 October 2023: the murder, rape and abduction of civilians.

10. Hamas is a terrorist organization. Its military and governing capabilities will be dismantled. It will not rule the Gaza Strip and will not threaten the citizens of Israel. Israel keeps its freedom of security action, in coordination with the international framework for Gaza. The government will act to entrench the end of the war, to bring home the remaining hostages and the bodies of the fallen, and to allow humanitarian aid and reconstruction for the civilian population.

11. Jewish terror. The government will fight Jewish terror and nationalist violence against Palestinians and their property with the same determination with which the state fights all terror. Its tools will include administrative detention under law and judicial review, a joint Shin Bet and police unit, the re-establishment of the Judea and Samaria police district, the dismantling of violent hubs set up without permission, and prosecution. This violence is the work of a small, violent minority. It harms Palestinians, the residents of the area and the State of Israel itself.

12. Within 14 days of being sworn in, the government will decide, under the Commissions of Inquiry Law, 5729–1968, to establish a state commission of inquiry into the events of 7 October, the decade that preceded them and the conduct of the war.

### Judea and Samaria (the West Bank)

13. In Judea and Samaria, which have been held under the authority of the military commander since 1967, the government will act as follows during its term:
   - a. There will be no annexation, no application of sovereignty and no change in legal status.
   - b. Powers transferred from the military commander to political appointees will be returned to the IDF and the Civil Administration.
   - c. No new communities will be established. Construction in existing communities will take place within their approved plans and by government decision.
   - d. The state budget will include no allocation to unauthorized outposts and no coalition money for them.

14. Outposts and "farms" established without a government decision and without legal authorization will be evacuated, beginning with those on private Palestinian land. A timetable will be published within 90 days, and a progress report will be published every quarter. No unauthorized outpost will be regularized during the term, apart from regularization steps decided by the government before 27 October 2026. Those steps will proceed under law and judicial review, and none of them on private Palestinian land.

15. Israel keeps full security responsibility and full freedom of security action throughout the area. The government will maintain security and civil coordination with the Palestinian Authority and will work with regional and international partners for stability. In accordance with clause 2, no decision on a permanent settlement will be taken during the term, and the parties' positions on such a settlement are fully reserved.

16. The government will preserve the historic status quo at the holy places in Jerusalem, including on the Temple Mount (al-Aqsa).

17. The commitments in the open letter of the Prime Minister-designate (Part B) will be adopted as a government decision at the government's first meeting and funded in the first state budget.

## Part B. Open letter of the Prime Minister-designate to the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads

*To be published before the investiture vote and annexed to the basic guidelines.*

To the Chair of the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads

Copy: the Chair of the High Follow-Up Committee

Dear Sir,

The government I am forming will act for all citizens of Israel. I am setting out here, publicly, what it will do for Arab society over the next four years. These are commitments to citizens and to the local leaders they elected, not a price paid for votes, and I ask you to hold us to them every quarter.

1. Crime and violence.
   - A national plan of at least NIS 1 billion a year, written into the budget baseline for four years.
   - It covers policing, prevention and enforcement against criminal organizations. This includes the Shin Bet, acting under law and judicial review and against those organizations only.
   - It will be run by a government coordinator for the fight against crime and violence in Arab society, appointed by government decision for the full term. The coordinator will hold written powers over the plan's budget, delegated by the minister responsible for the police, and may be removed only by the government.
   - The government intends to appoint Yoav Segalovitz to the post. Before taking it up he will resign from the Knesset and state publicly, in his own name, that he accepts the principles of the basic guidelines, among them clauses 3 and 10.
   - Every year the government will publish the number of homicides and the share of cases solved and brought to indictment.

2. Economic plan.
   - A five-year plan to succeed Government Decision 550, no smaller than it in real terms (about NIS 30 billion), approved in the first budget.
   - The cut grants to Arab local authorities will be restored.
   - Published targets for employment, Hebrew teaching and higher education, the placement of Arab teachers, medical residency posts, and housing and jobs for young people.

3. Planning and housing.
   - Master plans for Arab localities on a published timetable, and expanded jurisdictions.
   - From the day the government is sworn in, enforcement of Amendment 116 to the Planning and Building Law (the "Kaminitz Law") against existing inhabited homes will be frozen.
   - Within six months, an amendment will suspend the administrative fines and demolition orders against such homes until plans are approved, and will open a track for regularizing them. This includes the Triangle and the Negev, among them Tayibe, Tira, Qalansawe and the Negev localities.
   - Enforcement against new construction and against criminal organizations will continue.

4. The Negev.
   - A recognition and regularization process for the unrecognized villages, with recognition decisions on a first group within 18 months, and connection to electricity and water.
   - While the process runs, no inhabited home will be demolished. Where relocation is needed, it will take place only under a regularization plan agreed with the residents and their elected representatives.

5. Representation.
   - Targets for Arab representation in the civil service and on the boards of government companies.
   - The Knesset presidium and committees will be composed according to established practice and the size of each faction. The government parties will not block the election of a deputy Speaker from any faction.

6. Monitoring.
   - A monitoring committee chaired by the coordinator will meet monthly and publish a report every quarter.
   - Its members will be representatives of the government and of the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads.
   - Each Knesset faction that publicly answers this letter will name one MK to the committee. **[Not agreed. One option: "as a member with equal standing", demanded by Ra'am, Hadash and Ta'al and accepted by Yesh Atid and the Democrats. The other: "who will receive its reports and may put questions to it", demanded by Yashar and Yisrael Beiteinu.]**

7. The holy places and the way ahead.
   - The government will preserve the historic status quo at the holy places in Jerusalem, including on the Temple Mount (al-Aqsa).
   - It will maintain coordination with the Palestinian Authority, will take no step that precludes a future settlement between Israel and the Palestinians, and will respond to regional and international efforts toward stability and a settlement.

Yours sincerely,<br>Gadi Eisenkot, MK<br>Prime Minister-designate

## Part D. Replies of the Arab factions (unilateral; the government is not a party)

**Ra'am**
- Will vote for the investiture of the government and for budgets that fund Part B.
- Withdraws its support if the status quo at al-Aqsa is breached, or if a breach of Part B is not remedied within 60 days.
- Keeps a free vote on matters of religion and state, conscience and security, and does not adopt clauses 3 and 10 of Part A.
- Regards a failure to appoint the coordinator, or the coordinator's removal without cause, as a material breach of Part B.

**The Joint List (Hadash, Ta'al, Balad)**
- "We did not vote for this government." Hadash and Ta'al will abstain on investiture; Balad's MKs will be absent.
- While Part B is being implemented, the list will be a safety net for Arab society and will not support motions of no confidence or bills to dissolve the Knesset.
- The safety net lapses if a breach of Part B, or of the status quo at al-Aqsa, is not remedied within 60 days.
- Balad will be absent from budget votes only if the budget contains no money for annexation or outposts and funds Part B; otherwise its MKs vote against.
- Each component keeps its own positions:
  - Hadash on the occupation, the illegality of the settlements, the right of the Palestinian people to self-determination and to an independent state on the 1967 borders;
  - Ta'al on the occupation, the illegality of the settlements, the right of the Palestinian people to an independent state on the 1967 borders with East Jerusalem as its capital, and its rejection of administrative detention;
  - Balad on the occupation and the settlements;
  - all three in rejecting military and civil service in any form.
