# Four Years of Repair: Framework of Principles for the Government of Israel, 2026–2030

**Government parties:** Yashar · Together (Bennett, Yesh Atid) · Yisrael Beiteinu · The Democrats
**Parliamentary agreement:** Ra'am
**Safety-net declaration (Annex 1):** Hadash · Ta'al · Balad

---

## Preamble

1. This framework records principles agreed by parties with different worldviews **for one Knesset term (2026–2030)** only.
   Its purpose is to replace the outgoing government, restore the functioning of the state after 7 October, and repair what can be repaired in four years.

2. The government will be formed by Yashar, Together, Yisrael Beiteinu and The Democrats, headed by Gadi Eisenkot. It will rest on a parliamentary agreement with Ra'am (Chapter E). It will serve all citizens of Israel, Jews and Arabs alike.
   Matters not addressed here, such as religion and state, will be settled in the coalition agreement among the government parties.

3. The parties hold different, sometimes opposite, positions on long-term questions:
   - the final status of the territories;
   - the constitutional arrangement of the state;
   - the national aspirations of its citizens.

   This framework **does not decide these questions, does not prejudice them, and no party waives its positions on them**. During the term, no irreversible step will be taken in these matters.
   This does not prevent the government from protecting the independence of the judiciary, the Attorney General and the gatekeepers. Any change to the judicial system or constitutional process will be made only with broad consent.

## Chapter A: The state and its citizens

4. **Jewish and democratic state.**
   - The State of Israel is defined in its Basic Laws as a **Jewish and democratic state**.
   - The government will act within this framework and in the spirit of the **Declaration of Independence**, which promises "complete equality of social and political rights to all its inhabitants irrespective of religion, race or sex."
   - This clause describes the existing law. No party is thereby taken to endorse, or to renounce its position on, any particular Basic Law, including Basic Law: Israel – the Nation-State of the Jewish People. Each party keeps its freedom to act lawfully regarding it.

5. **Arab citizens.** The Arab citizens of Israel are equal citizens. Their elected representatives are a legitimate part of Israeli democracy, and the government will act **for them and with them**, as for all citizens.

6. **Civil equality.** During the term the government will not initiate legislation that harms the civil equality of any group of citizens, and will not make civil rights conditional on political views. Government services will be fully available in Arabic.

6a. **Service for All.** Within its first year the government will enact a Service Law for All, based on equal burden:
   - every citizen serves the state, in the IDF or in civil-national service;
   - no exemption quotas and no blanket exemptions;
   - state benefits are tied to service: whoever serves is rewarded.

   The duty applies to all; implementation will be phased by track.
   For Arab citizens, during this term, service will be civilian, in their own communities, run with the Arab local authorities, and with no security link. It will expand by published annual targets, through **incentives and equal benefits, not sanctions**.
   On the civil-national track for Arab citizens set out in this clause, **no citizen will lose, or have reduced, any existing right, allowance or benefit for not serving**. Service adds benefits and does not condition existing ones. This protection does not apply to any other population.

## Chapter B: Terrorism and the security of all

7. **All terrorism.** The parties **condemn all terrorism and all deliberate harm to innocent civilians**, whoever the perpetrator and whoever the victim. They mourn all innocent civilians who have lost their lives since 7 October, Israelis and Palestinians alike, including in Gaza.

8. **The 7 October massacre.** The parties condemn without reservation the **massacre carried out by Hamas on 7 October 2023**: the murder, rape and abduction of civilians.

9. **Hamas and the end of the war.**
   - **Hamas, a terrorist organization under Israeli law, will be disarmed and will not rule Gaza** or threaten the citizens of Israel.
   - The government will act to that end in coordination with the international framework for Gaza.
   - It will also act to:
     - consolidate the end of the war and prevent its resumption;
     - return any remaining hostages and the bodies of the fallen;
     - enable humanitarian aid and reconstruction for the civilian population, in accordance with international law.

10. **Jewish terror.**
    - The government will fight Jewish terror and nationalist violence against Palestinians and their property **with the same determination and the same tools used against Palestinian terror**.
    - These tools include:
      - administrative detention under law and judicial review;
      - a dedicated Shin Bet and police unit;
      - dismantling of illegal violent hubs;
      - prosecution.
    - This violence is perpetrated by a small extremist minority that harms Palestinians, the residents of the area and the State of Israel itself.

11. **Commission of inquiry.** At its first meeting, the government will establish a **state commission of inquiry** into:
    - the events of 7 October;
    - the decade preceding them;
    - the conduct of the war.

## Chapter C: The territories under military control

12. **Status of the territories.** Judea and Samaria / the West Bank are territories held under **military control** since 1967.

13. **No annexation.**
    - During the term there will be **no annexation, application of sovereignty or change in the legal status** of the territories.
    - The powers transferred from the military commander to political appointees will be returned to the IDF and the Civil Administration.
    - In existing communities, planning and construction will continue only in accordance with the law and government decisions.
    - The state budgets will include **no allocations to unauthorized outposts and no coalition funds**.

14. **Unauthorized outposts.**
    - Outposts and "farms" established without a government decision and without legal authorization, first and foremost those on private Palestinian land, **will be evacuated** according to a timetable published within 90 days.
    - **No unauthorized outpost will be legalized during the term.** Only regularization steps already decided by a government decision before 27 October 2026 may proceed, under law and judicial review, and none on private Palestinian land.
    - Progress on the evacuation timetable will be published quarterly.

15. **Security and the future settlement.**
    - Israel retains full security responsibility and freedom of operational action against terrorism throughout the area.
    - The government will maintain security and civil coordination with the Palestinian Authority, and will work with regional and international partners to stabilize the area.
    - In accordance with clause 3, no final-status decision will be taken during the term. The parties' positions on a future settlement between Israel and the Palestinian people are fully reserved.

15a. **Holy places.** The government will preserve the historic **status quo at the holy places** in Jerusalem, including the Temple Mount / al-Haram al-Sharif (al-Aqsa).

## Chapter D: A government that acts for Arab citizens (four-year commitments)

*Chapter D is a commitment of the government to all Arab citizens, not a price for votes.*

16. **National emergency plan against crime and violence** in Arab society:
    - at least **NIS 1 billion per year**, multi-year and protected from cuts;
    - it covers policing, prevention and enforcement against criminal organizations, involving all relevant state bodies, including the Shin Bet against criminal organizations, under law and judicial oversight;
    - it will be run with the Arab local authorities and led by the Deputy Minister named in clause 22;
    - an annual report on homicides and solved cases will be published.

17. **A successor five-year economic plan** to Government Decision 550:
    - **no smaller in real terms** than its predecessor (about NIS 30 billion);
    - approved in the first budget;
    - including the restoration of grants to Arab local authorities that were cut;
    - with published targets for:
      - employment;
      - Hebrew-language and higher education;
      - placement of Arab teachers;
      - medical residency slots;
      - a youth plan for housing, higher education and jobs.

18. **Planning and housing.**
    - Master plans for Arab localities will be prepared on a published timetable, and their jurisdiction will be expanded.
    - Enforcement of the Kaminitz Law against **existing inhabited homes** is frozen immediately.
    - Within 6 months, an amendment will cancel the administrative fines and administrative demolition orders against such homes, pending approved plans, including in the Triangle and the Negev.
    - Enforcement against new illegal construction (built after the government is sworn in) and against criminal organizations will continue.

19. **The Negev.**
    - A recognition and regularization process for the unrecognized villages, with recognition decisions on a first group within 18 months, in agreement with the residents.
    - Connection of these villages to electricity and water.
    - During the process there will be **no demolition of inhabited homes and no forced relocation** in these villages.

20. **Representation.**
    - Targets for Arab representation in the civil service and on the boards of public companies.
    - A deputy Speaker of the Knesset from each of the two Arab lists, Ra'am and the Joint List.
    - The chair of at least one Knesset committee.

## Chapter E: Parliamentary agreement with Ra'am

21. **Ra'am's votes.**
    - Ra'am will vote in favor of the government's investiture and its budgets, and will not support motions of no confidence or Knesset-dissolution bills.
    - Ra'am will vote in favor of the Service Law for All as set out in clause 6a. This vote is conditional on the bill including the provisions of clause 6a in full, among them the civil-national track for Arab citizens and the protection of existing rights.
    - On all other matters, Ra'am keeps freedom of vote.

22. **Deputy Minister.**
    - Ra'am's nominee, with the approval of the Prime Minister, will serve as **Deputy Minister for the fight against crime and violence in Arab society**.
    - The post sits in the ministry responsible for policing, under the responsible minister and government policy, outside the cabinet and without a vote in the security cabinet.
    - Ra'am will also chair one Knesset committee.

23. **Knesset Speaker.** The parties will support the election of a new Knesset Speaker in the first week of the Knesset.

24. **Monitoring committee.**
    - It will meet monthly and publish a quarterly report on the implementation of Chapter D.
    - Its members are the government, Ra'am and representatives of the National Committee of Arab Local Authority Heads.
    - Representatives of the factions that made the declaration in Annex 1 take part as observers and receive its reports.

25. **Disputes and breach.** Disputes will be brought to the monitoring committee. If a material breach of Chapter D is not remedied within 60 days, Ra'am is released from clause 21.

## Annex 1: Safety-net declaration

*A unilateral declaration by Hadash, Ta'al and Balad.*

The undersigned factions of the Joint List welcome the replacement of the outgoing government and the commitments of Chapter D.

They note in particular:
- clauses 7 and 10;
- clause 12;
- clause 13, as regards the prohibition of annexation, the return of powers, and the budget prohibitions;
- clause 14;
- clause 15a;
- clauses 16–20;
- the reference to the Palestinian people in clause 15.

They will not support motions of no confidence or Knesset dissolution against this government while Chapter D is being implemented. On every other vote they keep full freedom, including on defense and identity matters.

Their declaration does not constitute endorsement of any clause or part of a clause not listed above, including clauses 4 and 6a, nor of any Basic Law. The government is not a party to this declaration.

## Annex 2: Reservations recorded at signing

| Party | Reservation |
|---|---|
| **Together (Bennett)** | Keeps a free vote on nominees to the Knesset presidium (deputy Speakers). The four-year freeze in clauses 13–14 is not a waiver of its long-term positions (clause 3). |
| **Yisrael Beiteinu** | Will not count on the Joint List's votes, and says so publicly. |
| **The Democrats** | Read "existing communities" (clause 13) as communities lawfully established within their approved boundaries: no new communities, and no expansion through after-the-fact legalization. Will seek the ministry responsible for policing in the coalition agreement. |
| **Hadash** | Notes clauses 12–14 only as regards annexation and outposts. Regards all settlement in the territories occupied in 1967 as illegal. Noting clauses 10 and 16 does not mean accepting administrative detention or Shin Bet involvement in civilian policing. |
| **Ta'al** | Its reference to clauses 12–14 does not express support for settlement construction, which it regards as illegal under international law. Keeps its principled objection to administrative detention. |
| **Balad** | Abstains on investiture and budgets. Its abstention on each budget is conditional on the budget law respecting clause 13 and funding Chapter D; if either is breached, Balad votes against and its Annex 1 declaration lapses. |
