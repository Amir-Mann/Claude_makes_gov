# Hadash: round 1 review of draft v4 (run 2)

*Simulated negotiating team of Hadash, about 6 of the Joint List's 12 MKs. This is a simulation and does not speak for the real party.*

## 1. Structure

**Our ranking:**
1. **S1 with Annex 1[A] (an exchange of letters).** This is the Rabin model of 1992.
2. **S3 with the same letters to the Joint List.** Ra'am gets the credit inside, but we keep a written counterpart.
3. **S2.** "Policy, not a deal" is exactly the blank check Jabareen promised not to give ("There is no automatic support… certainly not to the opposition leadership", 103FM, 13.10).
4. **S4.**
   - A unity government with Likud, with or without Netanyahu, costs us nothing ideologically. We would lead the opposition with clean hands.
   - It betrays the 75% of Arab voters who turned out to remove "حكومة السفاحين".
   - We do not prefer S4. Ta'al and Ra'am would blame us for it.

**What our six MKs do at the investiture vote:**

| Scenario | Hadash vote | Standing commitment afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| S1 + letters + a horizon sentence (§2) | **Abstain, present in the hall** | Full safety net: no vote for no-confidence or dissolution while the letters are honoured |
| S1 + letters, no horizon anywhere | **Abstain**, to end Netanyahu's government | **No standing safety net.** No-confidence and budgets are judged vote by vote. |
| S1 or S3 + 15[B] in the public guidelines | **For**, as Hadash did for Rabin in July 1992 [bg] | Full safety net. We expect Eisenkot to decline this offer, but it is on the table. |
| S2 | **Abstain** on investiture only | None. Every budget and no-confidence vote is open. |
| S3 | **Abstain** | As in S1, depending on the letters |
| Any version with clause 13[B] | **Abstain on investiture; against on budgets** | None |

We never vote **against** a government that replaces Netanyahu, Ben-Gvir and Smotrich at investiture. We also never vote **for** a "Zionist government" that has no Palestinian horizon. We abstain openly, not by absence: our voters must see us bring Netanyahu down. Balad may choose to be absent. That is its right, and the arithmetic works either way: 55 for against 53 against.

## 2. Is the exchange of letters enough without a political horizon?

**No.** The letters are necessary, because they are the 1992 structure and they remove run 1's humiliation of a one-way declaration. They are not sufficient.

Jabareen called the horizon "the most important" of our four demands. Clause 15[A], "no final-status decision… between Israel and the Palestinians", is the opposite of a horizon.

**Minimum: one sentence in the PM-designate's letter to us.** It does not have to be in the public guidelines.

> "The government will maintain and strengthen security, civil and economic coordination with the Palestinian Authority, will take no step that precludes a future political settlement between Israel and the Palestinian people, and will respond positively to regional and international efforts to renew a diplomatic process with the Palestinian leadership."
>
> «ستعمل الحكومة على تعزيز التنسيق مع السلطة الفلسطينية، ولن تتخذ أي خطوة تحول دون تسوية سياسية مستقبلية بين إسرائيل والشعب الفلسطيني، وستتجاوب مع الجهود الإقليمية والدولية لتجديد مسار سياسي مع القيادة الفلسطينية.»

**Without this sentence:** we abstain once on investiture and offer no safety net.

**The letters must also:**
- be signed by the PM-designate and published;
- list Chapter D with its dates and clauses 5, 13, 14 and 15a;
- name the Joint List as the counterpart;
- release us from the safety net if a breach of Chapter D or clause 13/14 is not remedied within 60 days.

## 4. Gains and concessions, by our own worldview

**Gains:**
- Netanyahu, Ben-Gvir and Smotrich are out.
- No annexation, and outposts evacuated.
- Settler terror named.
- Kaminitz frozen; no Negev demolitions.
- About NIS 31bn plus NIS 1bn a year.
- The al-Aqsa status quo kept.
- A written counterpart, as in 1992.

**Concessions:**
- We tolerate an Eisenkot government that calls itself Zionist, affirms "Jewish and democratic" and promises to disarm Hamas.
- We record our objection to administrative detention and the Shin Bet role, but they stay in the text.
- No "occupation" in the government's own text.

**Verdict: ABSTAIN-TOLERATE.**
- It is a full safety net **only** with the horizon sentence.
- Without it, we abstain on investiture once, and nothing more.

## 6. Attacks the next morning, and our answers

- **Ra'am:** "Hadash got letters, we got a deputy minister."
  - Our answer: no party owns the crime plan (16[A]), and our letter binds the PM himself.
- **Balad and our base:** "You propped up the general of the Gaza war."
  - Our answer: we did not vote for him or sign his doctrine. We removed Ben-Gvir and stopped the demolitions.
- **Likud:** "A government of terror supporters."
  - Our answer: 75% of Arab citizens voted; their votes are not stolen.
- **Our Jewish left:** "No horizon."
  - Our answer: that is exactly why the horizon sentence is our condition.

## 7. Trades

- **With Yashar and Together:** they keep 4[A], "Zionist" in their own guidelines, and 15[A] in public. In return, the horizon sentence goes into our letter.
- **With the Democrats:** a joint push for 13[C] and 3[B].
- **With Ta'al:** Tibi gets the deputy Speaker post and the Finance Committee seat. Ta'al backs 16[A].
- **With Balad:** its budget absence is accepted, and Balad accepts a unified abstention on investiture.
- **With Ra'am:** we support Ra'am's equal seat on the monitoring committee if Ra'am accepts that the Joint List sits as an equal there too.
