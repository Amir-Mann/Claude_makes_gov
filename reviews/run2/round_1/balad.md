Simulated negotiating team. Our MKs are Awawdeh, Karkabi-Sabah and al-Nasasra. Party chair Abu Shehadeh is outside the Knesset after
being forced to withdraw. Source base: `research/balad.md`.

## 1. Structure

| Rank | Option | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **S2 (policy, not a deal)**, *if* clause 2 drops "Zionist" and clause 5 is strengthened | Balad signs nothing with a Zionist government, consistent with 2019, 2021 and 2022. Chapter D becomes policy the state owes Arab citizens, not a price for our votes. |
| 2 | **S1 with Annex 1 [A] letters** | Acceptable only if the PM-designate's letter is addressed to the whole Joint List and Balad's reservations are attached. Balad does not countersign any text that lists clauses 4, 6a, 9 or 12. |

| 4 | S4 (a Likud-without-Netanyahu unity government, or new elections) | **Honestly, this is not unbearable for Balad.** Opposition is our home and keeps our hands clean. We rank it last only because our voters turned out at about 80% "to bring down this government", and we do not want to be blamed for Netanyahu staying on as caretaker. |

**Investiture vote under our acceptable structure: ABSENT** (لن نكون حاضرين), not abstain and not for.
- Absence has the same arithmetic effect as abstention, but it is not a vote cast on a Zionist government.
- It is a single Joint List decision announced in advance.

### Could the Joint List split, with Balad voting against while Hadash and Ta'al stay absent or abstain?

**Yes, it could. It is a real option for us, not a bluff we have never used.**
- **Precedent for splitting:**
  - Sept 2019: Balad refused the recommendation of Gantz that 10 of the 13 Joint List MKs made.
  - May 2021: Balad refused to recommend Lapid while 5 of 6 did.
  - Sept 2022: Balad left the list over this very question.
- **Precedent for voting against:** June 2021. As we recall it, all six Joint List MKs, Abu Shehadeh included, voted *against* the Bennett–Lapid investiture, even though Netanyahu was the alternative. This is background knowledge; we could not re-verify it this session.
- **Arithmetic:**
  - Yes: Yashar, Together, the Democrats and Yisrael Beiteinu (48) plus Ra'am (7) = **55**.
  - No: the Netanyahu bloc (53) plus Balad (3) = **56**.
  - Hadash and Ta'al (9) absent or abstaining are not counted.
  - **The investiture fails, 55–56.** The same holds for every budget vote.
  - If only one Balad MK votes against, it is 55–54 and the government passes. All three of us are needed to block it.
- **When Balad would actually vote against:**
  - if the government advertises itself as a "Zionist government" that "does not rely on" Arabs while counting our absence;
  - if Yisrael Beiteinu's or Bennett's public boycott line stays;
  - if clause 5's anti-disqualification commitment is dropped;
  - if Balad's name appears under clauses 4, 6a or 9.

  We would pay a price with our voters for bringing Netanyahu back as caretaker. But being humiliated after our chair was disqualified costs us more, and our party congress would not accept it.
- **The likeliest real outcome** if our minimum is met: all three Balad MKs absent, together with the Joint List.

## 2. Clause by clause

- **2:** Delete "Zionist, statist". Keep "serves all citizens of Israel, Jews and Arabs alike". *This is a must-change for our absence.*
- **3:** Neutral. We prefer [B], repealing the 25th-Knesset judicial laws.
- **4:** **[B]**. [A] is the government parties' business. Balad records: «لا نعترف بتعريف الدولة كدولة يهودية».
- **5:** Must-change. The new wording must read:
  > "The government and its parties will not initiate or support legislation or petitions to widen the grounds for disqualifying lists or candidates, and will review section 7A of Basic Law: The Knesset with broad consent."
  >
  > «الحكومة وأحزابها لن تبادر أو تدعم تشريعًا أو التماسات لتوسيع أسباب شطب القوائم والمرشحين».

  These are the same parties that voted 31–4 to remove our chair.
- **6a:** **Reject.** Our platform: "We reject military and 'civil' service schemes, all forms of conscription."
  - 6a(c) "expands through published incentives" is still civil service for our youth.
  - Balad votes **against** the law, and our declaration must say so.
  - It is not a deal-breaker for our absence, as long as our tolerance is not tied to the law.
- **7–8:** Government text; Balad does not adopt it.
- **9:** We pick neither option; [B] is less bad. Balad does not note clause 9, nor the added "security freedom of action in Gaza".
- **10:** **[B]**. "קומץ אלים" is the government's framing, not ours; we call it «إرهاب المستوطنين» backed by the state.
- **11:** No objection.
- **12:** **[B]**. Under [A], "Judea and Samaria", clause 12 is removed from what we note.
- **13:** **[C]**, then [A]. [B] means our absence lapses at the first budget.
- **15:** **[B]**, "the Palestinian people" plus a diplomatic process.
- **16:** **[A]**, a coordinator recommended by the Arab local authorities. Our Shin Bet objection is recorded.
- **18:** **[B]**. [A] is acceptable only with "within 6 months" binding.
- **19:** **[B]**. Under [A], it must read "only within a plan **agreed with the residents**" («بموافقة السكان»).
- **20:** [A] is acceptable, since established practice gives the Joint List a deputy Speaker.
- **Annex 1:** **[A]**, provided that:
  - the letter is addressed to the Joint List;
  - Balad's paragraph reads "absent on investiture and budgets, conditional on clause 13 and Chapter D";
  - Balad's reservations on 4, 6a, 9 and 12 are recorded;
  - Yisrael Beiteinu's boycott line is struck. **Deal-breaker.**

## 6. Trades

- **With Hadash and Ta'al:** we back their "Palestinian people" and diplomatic-process demand in clause 15[B], and a bilateral letter. In return they back our clause 5 wording and the striking of Yisrael Beiteinu's boycott line, as a single Joint List condition.
- **With Yashar:** we keep quiet on clauses 4 and 9 being the government's own text. In return: clause 2 without "Zionist", plus clause 5.
- **With the Democrats:** clause 13[C] and 3[B].
- **Not tradeable:** absence turning into "abstain" or "for"; Balad's signature on 4, 6a or 9.
