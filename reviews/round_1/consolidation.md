# Round 1 consolidation

|---|---|---|

## Conflicts and how v2 resolves them
1. **Service law.** Five Zionist movements demand it; the Arab parties reject coercion.
   - v2: a Service Law for All, with no quotas or blanket exemptions and benefits tied to service.
   - For Arab citizens during this term the service is civilian and in their own communities, with no security link. It grows by published annual targets, driven by **incentives, not sanctions**.
   - Parties to the agreement keep a free vote on this law.
   - Bridges Yashar and Yisrael Beiteinu (YB) on one side and Ra'am and Hadash on the other.
2. **Hamas.** All the Arab signatories accepted "designated a terrorist organization under Israeli law". Ra'am refuses a *personal* pledge to "destroy" Hamas.
   - v2's joint text: "Hamas, a terrorist organization under Israeli law, will be disarmed and will not rule Gaza", with the government acting in coordination with the international framework.
   - Disarmament is the framework's own term, so it is not a pledge of destruction.
   - It also consolidates the end of the war and the return of hostages and bodies, which answers Balad, Hadash and Bennett.
   - Clause 7 now mourns civilians "Israelis and Palestinians alike, including in Gaza", which answers Balad and Hadash.
3. **Political horizon versus security freedom of action.** v2's clause 15 combines four elements:
   - security freedom of action (Yashar);
   - coordination with the Palestinian Authority (Democrats, Yesh Atid, Ta'al, Hadash);
   - no final-status decision (Yashar, Bennett);
   - positions on a future settlement "between Israel and the Palestinian people" reserved (Ta'al, Hadash).
   - "Political horizon" and "occupation" are dropped.
   - **Risk:** YB rejects the phrase "Palestinian people". It is tested in round 2.
4. **Settlement.** Bennett wants legal building to continue; the Democrats want no legalizations; Balad wants no settlement money.
   - v2: the status quo in existing communities, with planning only by law and government decision.
   - No legalization of outposts on private land; any other regularization only by government decision with judicial review.
   - No budgets for unauthorized outposts and no coalition funds; YB wants coalition funds cancelled anyway.
5. **Coalition shape.** YB wants Ra'am alone; the Democrats want "partnership"; Ra'am wants a role.
   - Arithmetic check: if Balad abstains (its stated posture), Zionists 48 + Ra'am 7 = 55, which beats 53 on investiture and budgets. Hadash and Ta'al's votes are therefore not needed.
   - v2's Chapter E is a parliamentary agreement with **Ra'am**, including a Ra'am nominee as Deputy Minister for fighting crime in Arab society. Ra'am suggested Segalovitz, a Jewish former police major-general and former Yesh Atid MK.
   - The Joint List (Hadash and Ta'al) gives a separate unilateral **safety-net declaration** (Annex 1), and Balad may join it. No one in the Joint List has to vote for anything.
   - Chapter D is framed as "a government commitment to all Arab citizens, not a price for votes" (YB's wording). That protects the Joint List's gains without making them a coalition partner.
   - Arab mayors sit on the monitoring committee.
   - The cabinet is not labelled "Zionist" or "partnership"; each party can describe it as it likes.
6. **Kaminitz, demolitions, Negev.** The bridge is **existing versus new** construction.
   - Enforcement against existing inhabited homes is frozen, and the amendment cancels administrative fines and orders on them.
   - Enforcement against new illegal building and against criminal organizations continues (Bennett, YB).
   - In Negev villages undergoing recognition there are no demolitions of inhabited homes and no forced relocation (Ra'am).
7. **Nation-State Law.** A neutral reservation is added: clause 4 describes the law; no signatory endorses or renounces its position on any particular Basic Law (Ta'al). Nothing is repealed (Bennett).
8. **Jewish terror.** v2 adds "the same determination and tools as against Palestinian terror, including administrative detention under law" (Democrats, Yashar, YB). It keeps "a small extremist minority" (YB, Bennett).
9. **Added at low cost to everyone:**
   - the al-Aqsa/Temple Mount status quo (Ra'am);
   - judicial independence and constitutional change only by broad consent (Yesh Atid);
   - the commission set up at the first government meeting, with the wider scope Yashar wants;
   - Shin Bet against criminal organizations (Bennett);
   - a youth plan plus teacher and doctor targets (Hadash, Ta'al), and employment and Hebrew-language targets (Yesh Atid);
   - Arabic in government services (Hadash, Balad);
   - a new Knesset Speaker;
   - a 60-day remedy period (Bennett);
   - religion and state left to the coalition agreement (YB).

## Not adopted
- "Belligerent occupation" (Hadash): unacceptable to all four Zionist lists. Hadash said it can live without it if "Palestinian people" appears.
- Ra'am as a formal "coalition member" label: replaced by the deputy-minister role.
- Arabic text without "يهودا والسامرة": Hadash's ordering is used instead, «الضفة الغربية (يهودا والسامرة في القانون الإسرائيلي)».
- Any Section 7A (candidate-disqualification) clause (Balad): too costly to Bennett and YB.
