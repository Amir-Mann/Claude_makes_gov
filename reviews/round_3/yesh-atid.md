## The clause 6a protection
The sentence reads: "On **this civil-national track**, no citizen will lose … any existing right … for not serving". The first bullet of clause 6a offers *every* citizen a choice between "the IDF or civil-national service".

So "this civil-national track" could be read as the general civil-national track. On that reading, a yeshiva student who chooses the civil track on paper keeps every existing benefit while not serving. That would nullify "state benefits are tied to service" for exactly the population our law targets. It would also break our core promise "מי שלא משרת — לא יקבל" ("whoever doesn't serve gets nothing").

The protection itself is fair for Arab citizens. Lapid has always supported incentive-based civil service in Arab society, and a phased Arab track is acceptable.

**Reservation, as an interpretive note recorded at signing:**
> Yesh Atid signs on the understanding that the protection in clause 6a applies only to **the civil-national track for Arab citizens** (HE: המסלול האזרחי־לאומי לאזרחים הערבים; AR: المسار المدني للمواطنين العرب) and does not apply to yeshiva students or any other population. Phased implementation by track will begin within the first year for all tracks.

**Preferred fix:** a single-word edit to the protection sentence, from "On this civil-national track" to "**On the civil-national track for Arab citizens**". This matches the consolidation's own stated intent, which scopes the protection to the Arab civil-national track, so it should cost no one anything. If the fix is made, our verdict becomes **SIGN**. Even if it is not, we sign with the reservation above rather than refuse.
