## 2. Gains vs concessions ledger (Yesh Atid's view)

**Gains:**
- Netanyahu is replaced.
- The Oct 7 commission is set up at the first meeting.
- **A Service Law for All within a year**, our flagship since 2013.
- The courts are protected.
- "Jewish and democratic" is anchored in the Declaration of Independence.
- Hamas is named a terror organization, disarmed and kept from ruling Gaza, and the hostages and fallen are returned.
- Jewish terror is fought "with the same tools" (Lapid's own line).
- No annexation, outposts are evacuated, and there is **no coalition funding** (כספים קואליציוניים).
- Lapid's 2021 Arab-citizen legacy is renewed with measurable targets.
- Security and civil coordination with the Palestinian Authority, which is consistent with Lapid's "day after" approach.

**Concessions, judged by the compromises made for others:**
- **A Ra'am deputy minister (clause 22).** This goes beyond pure outside support and strains Lapid's May 2026 undertaking to the AP ("no coalition with Ra'am"). It is mitigated because the post is outside the cabinet, limited to fighting crime, and reportedly filled by Yoav Segalovitz, a former Yesh Atid MK and police major-general. We can defend it as "a professional fighting crime".
- **"העם הפלסטיני" ("the Palestinian people"), clause 15.** No cost to us: Lapid spoke of "two states for two peoples" at the UN in 2022, and positions are reserved.
- **Mourning civilians "including in Gaza", clause 7.** Defensible, since the clause sits alongside the Hamas massacre and its disarmament.
- **The al-Aqsa / Temple Mount status quo, clause 15a.** This is official Israeli policy, so it costs us nothing.
- **The Kaminitz freeze on existing homes and no demolitions in Negev villages being recognised.** This is our 2021 precedent, and enforcement against new illegal construction continues.
- **No "ממשלה ציונית" ("Zionist government") label, clause 2.** A small loss. The cabinet is in fact all-Zionist, and we may describe it that way.
- **Religion and state are deferred to the coalition agreement.** Acceptable, but our "equal rights for every couple within 100 days" must be secured there.

**Net:** clearly positive. Our three must-haves (Netanyahu out, the commission, equal burden) plus courts and rule of law are all in the text. Our concessions are wording that Lapid has used himself, or our own 2021 policy.

## 3. Must-change items
None.

## 4. Nice-to-haves (not conditions)
1. **Clause 6a:** add "**Full-time yeshiva study is not service**" (HE: לימוד תורה במסגרת ישיבה אינו שירות). This closes the loophole of counting yeshiva study as civil service. If it is too sharp for the framework, it should go in the coalition agreement instead.
2. **Clause 22:** "appointed by the Prime Minister **on Ra'am's nomination**". This makes clear that the post is a government appointment.
3. **Clause 2:** "Matters not addressed here, such as religion and state **and civil marriage**…". This flags the issue for the coalition agreement.

## 5. Trade space
- We would accept dropping nice-to-haves 1 to 3 entirely.
- If Yisrael Beiteinu needs "the Palestinian people" softened, we are indifferent between that phrase and "the Palestinians". The parties for whom it matters are Hadash and Ta'al.
- We would resist weakening clause 6a, clause 3 or clause 11, which are our core gains.

## 6. Promise check
- **Kept:**
  - "Netanyahu out".
  - The Oct 7 commission "on the first day".
  - "שוויון בנטל וגיוס לכולם" ("equal burden and conscription for all").
