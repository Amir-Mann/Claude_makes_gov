## 2. Gains vs concessions (our worldview)

**Gains**
- Netanyahu out and Shas/UTJ out.
- **A Service Law for All in year one.** This is the law we brought down a government over in 2019.
- Oct 7 commission at the first meeting.
- **No coalition funds** (clause 13).
- A new Knesset Speaker (clause 23), which is our "replace Ohana" pledge.
- "מדינה יהודית ודמוקרטית" plus the Declaration of Independence.
- Hamas named a terror organization, to be disarmed and stripped of rule.
- Administrative detention for Jewish rioters, framed as "a small extremist minority".
- **Full operational freedom of action** throughout Judea and Samaria (clause 15).
- "יהודה ושומרון" is first in the Hebrew; no Palestinian state and no "כיבוש".
- Crime plan with the Shin Bet against crime organizations, which is our "טרור פלילי" line.
- Religion and state left to the coalition agreement among the Zionist parties.

**Concessions**
- The government rests on Ra'am's votes and **a Ra'am deputy minister**. This breaks "בלי רע"מ" and "no minority government" (15 Oct).
- "The Palestinian people" (clause 15).
- Mourning Gaza civilians (clause 7).
- Incentives rather than sanctions for Arab service this term.
- Kaminitz freeze on existing homes and no Negev demolitions during recognition.
- No sovereignty or annexation for 4 years. This costs us little; we never demanded formal annexation.

**On the other parties' compromises**
- **"Palestinian people" (cl. 15):** tolerable, because it sits in the "positions fully reserved" sentence and endorses nothing. Our position remains that any settlement runs through Jordan and the region (nice-to-have 1).
- **Clause 7 (Gaza):** acceptable. It says "innocent" civilians, it sits under "condemn all terrorism", and clause 8 fixes responsibility for the massacre on Hamas.
- **Ra'am deputy minister:** the price is high, but the portfolio is crime, which is our agenda, and it sits outside the cabinet. We are much easier with the reported nominee, a Jewish former police major-general.
- **Incentives instead of sanctions for Arabs:** acceptable *this term*. The principle of service for all is written into law, and the Haredim (our target) carry equal burden.

**Net:** positive. We deliver the draft law, no Haredim, no Netanyahu, the commission and Hamas. The text concedes nothing on "occupation" or a Palestinian state. The promise cost is coalition shape, and the alternative (new elections at 7 seats, or waiting for a Likud revolt that has not come) is worse.

## 3. Must-change (only one, and it is decisive)

**Clause 21: the Service Law cannot be hostage to Ra'am's free vote.** The arithmetic: 48 coalition votes against 53 opposing. If Ra'am abstains, our flagship law fails, while Ra'am's own gains in Chapter D are guaranteed. Replace the second sentence of clause 21 with:

> "Ra'am will vote in favor of the Service Law for All (HE: חוק שירות לכולם; AR: قانون الخدمة للجميع) as set out in clause 6a, including its track for Arab citizens. Otherwise it keeps freedom of vote."

This is a fair trade: the Arab track is civilian, community-based and incentive-only, as Ra'am asked.

## 4. Nice-to-have

1. **Clause 15:** "a future settlement between Israel, **the Palestinian people and the states of the region**" (HE: העם הפלסטיני ומדינות האזור; AR: الشعب الفلسطيني ودول المنطقة).
2. **Clause 9:** after "prevent its resumption", add "**while Israel retains freedom of action against any threat from Gaza**" (HE: תוך שמירת חופש הפעולה של ישראל מול כל איום מעזה).
3. **Clause 6a:** add "the duty of service applies to all; implementation will be phased by track". This protects the law from a Haredi "discrimination" petition to the High Court.

## 5. Trade space

- We drop all three nice-to-haves before we drop the must-change.
- We accept the Ra'am deputy minister, the Kaminitz and Negev clauses and "Palestinian people" as drafted.
- Timing: we will keep the Likud-without-Netanyahu door open until the President's consultations. If no Likud MKs defect, we sign.
